Seven Questions
1. Is the form geometrically symmetric?
BILATERALLY YES. Left-right mirror produces an identical form. Vertical mirror does not (V-dip at top, open bottom). The mark has a clear vertical directionality but perfect horizontal symmetry.
2. Is it perceptually asymmetric?
NO. The form is perceptually symmetric left-right. There is no visual weight imbalance, no angular offset, no stroke variation. The eye reads it as a balanced, centered form. The previous claim of "perceptual asymmetry through V-dip angle" is not supported — the V-dip angle is symmetric.
3. Does the V-dip itself provide sufficient identity?
YES. The V-dip is the defining recognition feature. At all sizes, the notch at the top of the mark creates a distinctive silhouette that is immediately recognizable. The V-dip depth (350u) and the two-stem-plus-notch structure are sufficient for identity without requiring lateral asymmetry.
4. Does the mark remain more specific than a generic M?
YES. A generic M would have angled diagonals meeting at a lower point, or a flat top. The V-dip — a sharp notch at the very top of two vertical stems — is not a standard M construction. It reads as a structural identity mark that happens to reference M, not as a typographic M.
5. Does mirroring destroy or preserve identity?
PRESERVES. Horizontal mirror produces an identical form — identity is fully preserved. Vertical mirror inverts the V-dip (notch at bottom instead of top), which changes the reading but the V-dip structure remains recognizable. The mark's identity survives mirroring in both axes.
6. Does the mark accidentally become V/W/Lambda-like?
AT 16px: The two vertical stems are clearly visible, preventing a pure V or Lambda reading. The V-dip is a notch between two verticals, not a standalone V shape. W is excluded because W has an inverted structure (pointing down). The mark reads as "two verticals with a V-notch" — not as any of these letters alone.
7. Does it remain visually related to the parent LUMORA M?
YES. The M-MICRO is a direct extraction of the wordmark M's structural essence: two vertical masses connected by a V-shaped relationship. The lineage is literal, not symbolic. When shown alongside the wordmark, the M letterform's V-dip is clearly visible as the micro-mark's defining feature.
